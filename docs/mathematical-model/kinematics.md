# Kinematics of the Axioma 4WD Robot

> Every parameter in this document is read from `model.sdf`,
> `axioma.urdf.xacro` and `nav2_params.yaml`. The diagram in
> `images/mathematical-model.png` is generated by `render_diagram.py`, which
> reads those same files, so it cannot fall out of step with this text.

## 1. Introduction

Axioma is a **four-fixed-wheel skid-steer**: there is no steering, it turns by
driving the two sides at different speeds. It is controlled as a differential
drive:

- The left wheels (W1 front, W2 rear) turn together
- The right wheels (W3 rear, W4 front) turn together

The difference between sides produces the rotation. Section 6 explains why that
equivalence with a differential drive **is not exact** and what has to be done
to keep the odometry from drifting.

## 2. Geometry

### 2.1 Wheel positions

Joint origins in the `base_link` frame (`axioma.urdf.xacro`, identical in
`model.sdf`):

| Wheel | Name | $(x, y, z)$ [m] | Joint |
|-------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| W1 | Front left | (0.06442, 0.07385, 0.04068) | `base_to_wheel1` |
| W2 | Rear left | (-0.071224, 0.07385, 0.04068) | `base_to_wheel2` |
| W3 | Rear right | (-0.071224, -0.0625, 0.04068) | `base_to_wheel3` |
| W4 | Front right | (0.064423, -0.0625, 0.04068) | `base_to_wheel4` |

The robot is **not symmetric** about `base_link`: the left wheels are at
$+0.07385$ m and the right ones at $-0.0625$ m. The real axis of rotation ends
up $5.7$ mm to the left of the frame origin. That is negligible next to the map
resolution (50 mm), but worth knowing.

### 2.2 Three different track widths

This is where it is easy to go wrong, because there are **three** numbers and
only one works in the equations:

| Concept | Symbol | Value | What it is |
|----------|---------|-------|--------|
| Joint track | $W_j$ | 0.13635 m | $y_{left} - y_{right}$ of the joint origins |
| Contact track | $W_c$ | 0.17635 m | The same, but in the contact plane of the collision cylinder, offset $+0.02$ m in local $y$ |
| **Effective track** | $W$ | **0.1679 m** | The one that goes in the equations; calibrated, see section 6 |

$$
W_j = 0.07385 - (-0.0625) = 0.13635 \text{ m}
$$
$$
W_c = W_j + 2 \times 0.02 = 0.17635 \text{ m}
$$

**Wheelbase**:
$$
L = 0.06442 - (-0.071224) = 0.13564 \text{ m}
$$

### 2.3 Real footprint

Measured on the visual meshes (`chasis.stl` + 4 × `llanta.stl` placed at their
joints):

$$
\text{length} = 0.2166 \text{ m}, \qquad
\text{width} = 0.2220 \text{ m}, \qquad
\text{height} = 0.1548 \text{ m}
$$

The circumscribed radius from `base_link` is **0.1577 m**, which is what fixes
`robot_radius: 0.16` in the costmaps.

### 2.4 Coordinate frame

```
        y (left)
        ↑
        |     W2 ●━━━━━● W1        x forward
        |        |     |           y left
        |        | {R} |           z up
        |        |     |           θ counter-clockwise
        |     W3 ●━━━━━● W4
        └─────────────────→ x (forward)
```

The LiDAR is at $(0, 0, 0.15)$: exactly **above** the origin of `base_link`,
with no lateral or longitudinal offset.

### 2.5 `base_footprint`

The root of the URDF is `base_footprint`, the projection of `base_link` onto the
ground. It exists for two reasons:

1. **KDL does not accept inertia on the root link.** With `base_link` as root,
   `robot_state_publisher` warned on every startup. `base_footprint` carries no
   `<inertial>`, so the warning is gone.
2. **It is the honest frame for the odometry.** The plugin publishes planar
   odometry with $z = 0$, which is the ground, not the origin of `base_link`.

$$
\mathbf{T}^{footprint}_{base} = \text{translation}(0,\ 0,\ -0.00258)
$$

The 2.58 mm are not arbitrary: the wheel centre is at $z = 0.04068$ and the
radius is $0.0381$, so with the wheels resting, `base_link` ends up
$0.0381 - 0.04068 = -0.00258$ m **below** the ground. Gazebo reports exactly
that $z$ for the model.

Resulting tree:

```
map ──(AMCL)──► odom ──(EKF)──► base_footprint ──(URDF)──► base_link
                                                              ├─► base_scan
                                                              ├─► imu_link
                                                              ├─► zed2i_camera_link
                                                              └─► wheel_1..4
```

`robot_base_frame` in Nav2 and `base_frame` in SLAM Toolbox both point at
`base_footprint`. `odom -> base_footprint` has exactly one publisher, selected
by `simulation.launch.py`: the EKF in `axioma_perception` by default,
`odom_to_tf` when `use_ekf:=false`, or the visual odometry when the VSLAM demo
runs with `publish_odom_tf:=false`.

## 3. Differential drive model

### 3.1 Body twist

$$
\mathbf{v}_R = \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}
$$

A differential drive has **no lateral mobility**: $v_y = 0$. That is why
`vy_samples` is 1 and `max_vel_y` is 0 in the DWB configuration.

### 3.2 Wheel grouping

$$
\omega_L = \omega_{W1} = \omega_{W2}, \qquad
\omega_R = \omega_{W3} = \omega_{W4}
$$

---

## 4. Forward kinematics

$$
v = \frac{r(\omega_R + \omega_L)}{2}, \qquad
\omega = \frac{r(\omega_R - \omega_L)}{W}
$$

**Derivation**. The linear velocity is the average of the two sides:

$$
v = \frac{v_R + v_L}{2} = \frac{r\omega_R + r\omega_L}{2}
$$

and the angular one comes from the difference spread over the track:

$$
\omega = \frac{v_R - v_L}{W} = \frac{r(\omega_R - \omega_L)}{W}
$$

**Matrix form**, with $r = 0.0381$ m and $W = 0.1679$ m:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} r/2 & r/2 \\ -r/W & r/W \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \omega_L \\ \omega_R \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} 0.01905 & 0.01905 \\ -0.22692 & 0.22692 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \omega_L \\ \omega_R \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 5. Inverse kinematics

$$
\omega_L = \frac{v - \omega W/2}{r}, \qquad
\omega_R = \frac{v + \omega W/2}{r}
$$

**Matrix form**:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \omega_L \\ \omega_R \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} 1/r & -W/(2r) \\ 1/r & W/(2r) \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} 26.2467 & -2.20341 \\ 26.2467 & 2.20341 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}
$$

---

## 6. Why $W$ is not a measurable distance

### 6.1 The problem

The equations above describe an ideal differential drive: two wheels that only
roll. A four-fixed-wheel skid-steer **cannot turn without scrubbing its wheels
sideways**, and that scrub opposes the yaw. The result is that the body turns
**less** than the ideal model predicts.

The plugin uses the same $W$ for two things:

1. Converting $(v, \omega)$ from `/cmd_vel` into wheel velocities
2. Integrating the wheel velocities to publish `/odom`

If $W$ does not match the vehicle's **effective** track, the odometry publishes
a rotation the robot did not make. And unlike translation error, this error
rotates the whole frame: it accumulates without bound.

### 6.2 How much

Measured against Gazebo's ground-truth pose, with the original geometric track
($W = 0.1725$ m) and isotropic friction:

| Commanded $\omega$ | Real rotation | Rotation per odometry | Ratio |
|--------------------|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| 0.30 rad/s | 56.4° | 86.0° | 0.656 |
| 0.50 rad/s | 115.6° | 172.4° | 0.670 |
| 0.80 rad/s | 106.4° | 172.1° | 0.618 |
| 1.00 rad/s | 101.9° | 172.4° | 0.591 |

The robot turned around **65 %** of what was commanded, and the ratio also
**depended on speed**. In a straight line the odometry was exact.

### 6.3 The two corrections

**a) Anisotropic friction.** In `model.sdf` the wheels had $\mu = \mu_2 = 1$.
With that isotropic friction, lateral scrub resists as much as rolling does.
The fix is to tell ODE/DART which direction the friction is along:

```xml
<fdir1>0 0 1</fdir1>   <!-- cylinder axis = wheel axle -->
<mu>0.05</mu>          <!-- lateral: scrub nearly free -->
<mu2>1.0</mu2>         <!-- rolling: traction intact -->
```

`fdir1` points along the **wheel axle**, the only direction of the body that
does not rotate when the wheel turns. That leaves $\mu$ as the lateral
coefficient and $\mu_2$ as the rolling coefficient.

> Lowering $\mu$ or $\mu_2$ **without** declaring `fdir1` achieves nothing: the
> physics engine picks an arbitrary direction, and all you get is lost traction.

**b) Calibrated effective track.** With the friction corrected a residual bias
remains, and it is removed by adjusting $W$:

$$
W = \frac{W_c}{k}, \qquad k = \frac{\omega_{real}}{\omega_{cmd}}\bigg|_{W = W_c} = 1.05
$$
$$
W = \frac{0.17635}{1.05} = 0.1679 \text{ m}
$$

### 6.4 Result

| Commanded $\omega$ | Ratio before | Ratio now |
|--------------------|-------------|-------------|
| 0.10 rad/s | 1.208 | 0.992 |
| 0.20 rad/s | 1.082 | 0.992 |
| 0.30 rad/s | 1.040 | 1.015 |
| 0.50 rad/s | 1.064 | 0.973 |
| 0.80 rad/s | 1.092 | 1.003 |
| 1.00 rad/s | 1.026 | 0.994 |

Yaw error **below 3 %** across the whole range, with no remaining speed
dependence, and exact translation at any speed.

> Note for the physical robot: exactly the same reasoning applies. A
> skid-steer's effective track depends on the surface, so it has to be
> calibrated by spinning the robot a known number of turns and comparing
> against the odometry.

## 7. Odometry

### 7.1 Pose integration

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix}_W =
\begin{bmatrix} v\cos\theta \\ v\sin\theta \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}
$$

### 7.2 Numerical integration

With $\Delta t = 0.02$ s (`odom_publish_frequency` = 50 Hz):

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{k+1} &= x_k + v\cos\theta_k \, \Delta t \\
y_{k+1} &= y_k + v\sin\theta_k \, \Delta t \\
\theta_{k+1} &= \theta_k + \omega \, \Delta t
\end{aligned}
$$

This is planar by construction: it has no term that can raise $z$, which is why
a ramp is invisible to it. `axioma_perception` replaces the integration with an
EKF that carries the attitude the IMU measures.

### 7.3 Implementation

**Plugin**: `gz-sim-diff-drive-system` (Ignition Fortress). Not
`libgazebo_ros_diff_drive.so`: that one is Gazebo Classic's and is not used
here.

```xml
<plugin filename="gz-sim-diff-drive-system"
        name="gz::sim::systems::DiffDrive">
  <left_joint>base_to_wheel1</left_joint>
  <left_joint>base_to_wheel2</left_joint>
  <right_joint>base_to_wheel4</right_joint>
  <right_joint>base_to_wheel3</right_joint>
  <wheel_separation>0.1679</wheel_separation>
  <wheel_radius>0.0381</wheel_radius>
  <odom_publish_frequency>50</odom_publish_frequency>
  <topic>cmd_vel</topic>
  <odom_topic>odom</odom_topic>
  <frame_id>odom</frame_id>
  <child_frame_id>base_link</child_frame_id>
</plugin>
```

The plugin integrates from the **actual joint positions**, not from the
command. So if a wheel slips, the odometry believes it: the same behaviour an
encoder has on the real robot.

### 7.4 TF

The plugin publishes on the Gz `tf` topic, which is **not** bridged to ROS.
Instead, whichever node owns `odom -> base_footprint` publishes it with the
canonical frame names: the EKF in `axioma_perception` by default, or
`odom_to_tf` (`axioma_gazebo`), which subscribes to `/odom` and republishes it
verbatim, when `use_ekf:=false`.

The child is `base_footprint` and not `base_link` because `base_link` already
hangs off `base_footprint` through the URDF's fixed joint, and **a frame can
only have one parent**. Publishing `odom -> base_link` here would compete with
`robot_state_publisher` for it.

## 8. Constraints and limits

### 8.1 Body limits

The same in Nav2 and in the plugin:

$$
|v| \leq 0.26 \text{ m/s}, \quad
|\omega| \leq 1.0 \text{ rad/s}, \quad
|\dot v| \leq 1.0 \text{ m/s}^2, \quad
|\dot\omega| \leq 3.2 \text{ rad/s}^2
$$

### 8.2 Wheel limits

Substituting into the inverse kinematics:

| Case | Calculation | Result |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Straight | $v_{max}/r$ | 6.82 rad/s (65.2 RPM) |
| Pure turn | $\omega_{max} W/(2r)$ | 2.20 rad/s (21.0 RPM) |
| Combined | $(v_{max} + \omega_{max}W/2)/r$ | 9.03 rad/s |

### 8.3 Turning radius

$$
R_{min} = 0 \text{ m (turn in place)}, \qquad
R\big|_{v_{max},\,\omega_{max}} = \frac{0.26}{1.0} = 0.26 \text{ m}
$$

### 8.4 Braking distance

$$
d = \frac{v_{max}^2}{2|a_{min}|} = \frac{0.26^2}{2 \times 1.0} = 0.034 \text{ m}
$$

## 9. Reference implementation

```python
R = 0.0381     # wheel_radius   (model.sdf)
W = 0.1679     # effective wheel_separation (model.sdf, calibrated)
V_MAX, W_MAX = 0.26, 1.0


def inverse_kinematics(v, omega):
    """Body (v, omega) -> wheel angular velocities [rad/s]."""
    v = max(-V_MAX, min(V_MAX, v))
    omega = max(-W_MAX, min(W_MAX, omega))
    return (v - omega * W / 2) / R, (v + omega * W / 2) / R


def forward_kinematics(w_left, w_right):
    """Wheel angular velocities -> body (v, omega)."""
    return R * (w_right + w_left) / 2, R * (w_right - w_left) / W
```
